Medical Context

Understanding Chronic Kidney Disease

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) represents a gradual, irreversible decline in renal filtration capacity. Often called a "silent condition," symptoms rarely emerge until advanced stages.

The Silent Progression

Nephrons gradually lose function over months or years. Up to 90% of renal function can be compromised before overt clinical symptoms such as severe fatigue or peripheral edema present.

Key Risk Precursors

Hypertension and Diabetes Mellitus are the leading drivers of microvascular renal injury. Prolonged elevated arterial pressure and hyperglycemia degrade glomerular capillaries.

Early Intervention Window

When detected in Stages 1–3, dietary modification, blood pressure control, and ACE-inhibitor therapies can dramatically slow disease progression, preventing end-stage dialysis.

Community Impact

Why Routine Screening Matters in Primary Care

In rural Primary Health Centres (PHCs) and community diagnostic camps, access to nephrologists is severely constrained. Routine laboratory workups—such as dipstick urinalysis, serum creatinine, and complete blood counts—are frequently gathered but difficult to interpret holistically.

RenalCare bridges this diagnostic gap by aggregating 24 standard routine parameters through a trained machine learning pipeline. It flags high-risk patterns immediately, generating actionable clinical documentation for physician referral.

Clinical Screening Benefits

  • Zero-Missing-Barrier: Built-in statistical imputers handle incomplete laboratory tests typical of rural camps.
  • Prioritized Sensitivity: Hyperparameters are specifically tuned to maximize sensitivity and minimize false negatives during initial screening.
  • Calibrated Probability: Provides nuanced risk stratification rather than misleading binary outputs.
  • Printable Referral PDF: Clean medical report ready for patient handover and hospital transfer.
Operational Workflow

How the Screening Process Works

Designed for streamlined execution by primary care nursing officers, camp coordinators, and health workers.

01

Collect Routine Labs

Gather standard dipstick urinalysis, blood pressure, fasting/random glucose, and complete metabolic profile.

02

Enter Parameters

Input available values into the structured 24-feature form. Reference ranges and automated pre-checks guide the operator.

03

Model Evaluation

The machine learning pipeline scales, imputes, and assesses multi-parameter correlations in milliseconds.

04

Clinical Report & Action

Review the risk summary, inspect abnormal findings, and download a branded PDF report for specialist consultation.

Diagnostic Variables

24 Clinical Parameters Analyzed

Covering urinalysis, renal chemistry, hematology, cardiovascular vitals, and physical symptoms from the UCI benchmark.

Urinalysis Biomarkers

Specific Gravity (sg), Albumin / Proteinuria (al), Urine Sugar (su), Red Blood Cells (rbc), Pus Cells (pc), Pus Cell Clumps (pcc), Bacteria (ba).

Renal & Chemistry Panel

Serum Creatinine (sc), Blood Urea (bu), Blood Glucose Random (bgr), Serum Sodium (sod), Serum Potassium (pot), Resting Blood Pressure (bp).

Hematology & CBC

Hemoglobin (hemo), Packed Cell Volume / Hematocrit (pcv), White Blood Cell Count (wc), Red Blood Cell Count (rc).

Systemic Comorbidities

Hypertension history (htn), Diabetes Mellitus (dm), Coronary Artery Disease (cad).

Clinical Physical Signs

Reported Appetite level (appet), Pedal Edema swelling (pe), Clinical Anemia presentation (ane).

Patient Demographics

Patient age, sex, and institutional identifier for tracking screening history across clinical sessions.
